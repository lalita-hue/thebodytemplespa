The Longevity Approach

Designed so your natural lashes come out of this healthier.

A lash set is only as good as the lash line underneath it. We plan for the lashes you will still have in a year, not just how the set photographs today.

01

Map

We read your eye shape and your natural lash line before anything is applied — length, curl and weight chosen lash by lash. A set mapped to your eye opens it; a set applied one length across closes it.

02

Every extension is isolated onto a single natural lash and weight-matched to what that lash can carry. Non-toxic adhesives, a clean unrushed room, and nothing stuck to the skin or to its neighbours.

Maintain

Retention is half technique and half aftercare, so we walk you through yours properly. Fills every two to three weeks keep the set full — and keep your lash line growing the way it should.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

The things people ask before their first set.

Classic or volume — which set is mine?+
Classic places one extension on each natural lash. It lengthens and defines while still reading like your own lashes, and it is where most people start. Volume places a handmade fan of several ultra-fine extensions on each lash, for far more density and a darker lash line. Volume is not heavier — the fibres are finer, so the weight stays matched. If your natural lashes are sparse, volume fills the gaps better; if they are already full, classic may be all you need.
Will extensions damage my natural lashes?+
Not when they are applied correctly, and that is the whole reason we weight-match. Damage comes from extensions that are too long or too heavy for the lash carrying them, or from lashes glued together so they cannot shed on their own cycle. We isolate every natural lash and choose a length and thickness it can support — which sometimes means telling you a length will not work on you. Your own lash line should come out of a year of extensions unchanged.
How often do I need fills?+
Every two to three weeks. Natural lashes shed on their own cycle and take their extension with them, so a set thins gradually rather than falling out all at once. Coming in at two to three weeks means a fill; leaving it much longer means enough has grown out that you may need the longer 75-minute fill or a new full set. Booking your next fill before you leave is the cheapest way to stay full.
What is a Korean lash lift, and how is it different?+
A lash lift curls your own lashes rather than adding anything to them. The Korean method lifts from the root with a softer, more natural curve than a traditional lift, which suits straight, downward-pointing and stubborn lashes that refuse to hold a curl. It conditions them as it lifts and helps them grow. It lasts six to eight weeks with no fills and no upkeep, and it pairs with a lash tint if your lashes are pale. It is the right choice if you want your eyes open without a maintenance schedule.
How long does a first appointment take?+
Plan for an hour and a half for a classic full set and two hours for volume. A lash lift is an hour, and tints are fifteen to twenty minutes. You lie down with your eyes closed the whole time, so most people rest through it. Come with clean lashes and no eye makeup — anything left on the lash line weakens adhesion and costs you retention later.
Can I get a fill on someone else's set?+
Our fill pricing is for lashes applied here, because we know what is underneath them. If you are coming from another studio, book a full set instead and we will assess your lash line first — sometimes the existing work can be filled, and sometimes it needs to come off so your natural lashes can recover. We will tell you honestly which one you are looking at before we start.
How do I make my set last?+
Cleanse them daily with a lash-safe foaming cleanser — dirty lashes shed faster, which surprises most people. Avoid oil-based products around the eye, skip the mascara and the curler entirely, and try not to sleep face-down on them. We walk you through your aftercare properly at your first appointment, because retention is genuinely half technique and half what happens at home.
Who is not a candidate?+
Extensions are not suitable during an active eye infection, conjunctivitis or a stye, or on anyone with a known adhesive allergy. Recent eye surgery, lash-growth serums and certain eyelid conditions need discussion first, and lash lifts are not done during pregnancy. Tell us everything at intake — it changes what we recommend, not whether we will treat you kindly.
