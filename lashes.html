The Longevity Approach

Designed so your lashes come out of this stronger.

A lift is a chemical process. Done carelessly it costs you lashes; done properly it gives you curl and leaves the lash healthier than it was. The difference is entirely in the timing.

01

Lift

Rod size is chosen for your lash length and your eye shape, then each lash is set individually along it. Processing is timed to your own lash thickness rather than a standard clock — which is what keeps the curve soft instead of kinked.

02

Condition

A nourishing keratin step goes on straight after the lift, while the lash is still open — that is the window where it actually absorbs. Non-toxic throughout, and the reason lashes come back fuller rather than dry.

03

Maintain

Nothing to book in between. We walk you through the first twenty-four hours — the only part that can undo the work — and you come back in six to eight weeks when the curl softens.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

The things people ask before their first lift.

What is a Korean lash lift, and how is it different?+
A lash lift curls your own lashes rather than adding anything to them. The Korean method lifts from the root with a softer, more natural curve than a traditional lift, which suits straight, downward-pointing and stubborn lashes that refuse to hold a curl. It conditions them as it lifts and helps them grow. A traditional lift tends to curl the whole lash into a hook; this opens the eye without looking obviously done.
How long does it last?+
Six to eight weeks, which is roughly one full growth cycle — the curl does not drop out, it grows out. There are no fills and nothing to book in between, so you simply come back when it softens. Most people settle into every other month, and the lashes are in the same or better condition each time.
Will it damage my lashes?+
Not when the timing is right, and timing is the whole job. A lift is a chemical process, and solution left on too long is what leaves lashes frizzy, kinked or brittle. Fine lashes process much faster than coarse ones, so we judge yours as we go rather than following a standard clock — and the conditioning step goes on straight afterwards, while the lash is still open enough to absorb it.
Do I need the tint as well?+
If your lashes are dark to the tip, the lift alone will do it. If they are blonde, red-toned or fade to clear at the ends — which is more common than people realise — the lift will open your eye but the lashes will still read as sparse without mascara. The tint is what makes a lift look finished, and it is twenty minutes on the same visit. We will tell you honestly which camp you are in.
What do I do for the first 24 hours?+
Keep them dry and leave them alone. No water on the lashes, no steam, no sauna, no swimming, no mascara and no rubbing your eyes — the bonds are still setting, and this is the one part of the process you can undo yourself. Try to sleep on your back the first night. After twenty-four hours there is nothing to avoid, and a lash serum is fine.
Can I get a lift if I have extensions on?+
Not over them. Extensions have to come off and the natural lashes need to be clean and free of adhesive first, so plan a removal beforehand rather than the same appointment. A lift is a good next step if extensions have left your lashes thin — it gives you the open eye while your own lash line recovers, without anything sitting on it.
How short can my lashes be?+
Short lashes can absolutely be lifted — a lift makes them look longer, because a lash pointing up shows its full length while one pointing forward does not. Very short or very sparse lashes are the one case where the result may be subtle, and we will say so before you book rather than after. Send a photo if you want a straight answer first.
Who is not a candidate?+
Lash lifts are not done during pregnancy or breastfeeding, on an active eye infection, conjunctivitis or a stye, or on anyone with a known allergy to the solutions. Recent eye surgery, lash-growth serums and certain eyelid conditions need discussion first. Tell us everything at intake — it changes what we recommend, not whether we will treat you kindly.
