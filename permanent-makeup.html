FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

The things people ask before trusting someone with their face.

Which brow technique is right for me?+
It depends on your skin and the look you want. Microblading draws individual hair strokes for the most natural finish and suits normal to dry skin. Powder / Ombré is a soft shaded fill that holds on every skin type including oily, and reads like tinted brow makeup. Microshading sits between them. Your consultation settles it in about ten minutes — we look at your skin, your existing hair and how defined you want to look.
Does it hurt?+
Most clients describe it as scratching or pressure rather than sharp pain. Topical numbing is used throughout, and the light therapy and lymphatic drainage included in every service are there partly for comfort. Lips are generally more sensitive than brows. Tell us if you are having a tender week in your cycle — we can reschedule, since sensitivity is genuinely higher then.
How long does it last?+
Typically one to three years depending on the technique, your skin type, sun exposure and skincare. Oily skin and acid-based skincare fade pigment faster. Rather than a hard "redo," most clients come back for a refresh when the colour softens — and because we design for graceful fading, that in-between stage still looks intentional.
What does healing actually look like?+
Expect colour to look bolder and darker for the first several days, then lighten considerably as the skin flakes and settles — often looking too light around week two before the colour resurfaces. This is normal and it is exactly why the 6–8 week perfecting session exists. Judging the result before that point is judging an unfinished process.
Is the perfecting session required?+
Effectively, yes. Skin never heals perfectly evenly, so the 6–8 week session is where shape is refined and anything that healed light is filled. It is booked separately at $155 and it is a refinement rather than a redo — which is why it is a fraction of the original price. Plan for it when you plan your budget.
Can you fix or cover old permanent makeup?+
Often, but not always, and never sight unseen. Old work that has turned warm, grey or blue, or that was shaped badly, may need colour correction or removal before anything new goes on top. Book a consultation and bring photos of the original work if you have them — we will tell you honestly what is possible and what is not.
Who is not a candidate?+
Permanent makeup is not suitable during pregnancy or breastfeeding, on active skin infections or cold sore outbreaks, or for anyone on Accutane within the past year. Blood thinners, diabetes, keloid scarring and certain autoimmune conditions need discussion first. Tell us everything at intake — it changes what we recommend, not whether we will treat you kindly.
