Why this matters

Nothing moves until you open the pathway first

Most therapists learn lymphatic drainage the way most drivers learn a shortcut: secondhand. A video, a weekend workshop, a coworker who picked it up somewhere. It sort of works — until a client asks a question you cannot answer, or you are not sure whether that swelling is normal healing or something you should be referring out today.

Here is the thing about the lymphatic system: it has no pump of its own. Fluid only moves when you open the right pathway first. Push against a closed pathway and nothing drains, no matter how hard you work.

And it is not water you are moving.

Almost everyone calls it water retention. It is not. It is fluid retention, and that fluid is a great deal more than water — plasma proteins too large for the blood capillaries to take back, cellular waste, bacteria, immune cells. That is why it sits there, and it is why moving it takes a sequence rather than pressure.

The distinction is not trivia. Your clients research this before they book. Every time you say water retention to a client who has done their homework, you sound like someone who took a weekend course. Say fluid retention, explain what is actually in it and where you are sending it, and you sound like the expert they were looking for.
