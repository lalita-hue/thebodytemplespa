Why this matters

Nothing moves until you open the pathway first

Most therapists learn lymphatic drainage the way most drivers learn a shortcut: secondhand. A video, a weekend workshop, a coworker who picked it up somewhere. It sort of works — until a client asks a question you cannot answer, or you are not sure whether that swelling is normal healing or something you should be referring out today.

Here is the thing about the lymphatic system: it has no pump of its own. Fluid only moves when you open the right pathway first. Push against a closed pathway and nothing drains, no matter how hard you work.

And it is not water you are moving.

Almost everyone calls it water retention. It is not. It is fluid retention, and that fluid is a great deal more than water — plasma proteins too large for the blood capillaries to take back, cellular waste, bacteria, immune cells. That is why it sits there, and it is why moving it takes a sequence rather than pressure.

The distinction is not trivia. Your clients research this before they book. The moment you say fluid retention instead of water retention, and can explain what is actually in it and where you are sending it, you stop sounding like someone who took a weekend course and start sounding like the expert they were looking for.

Every module builds on the one before it, so by the end you do not just recognize lymphatic drainage — you can perform it, explain it and defend the clinical decision behind it.

1
Foundations of Lymphatic Drainage
Where manual lymphatic drainage came from, why it works, and the real difference between a wellness session and a post-operative one — the “why” before you ever touch a client.
2
Lymphatic Anatomy & Physiology
Nodes, vessels, watersheds and the terminus. The map you need before you can safely move fluid anywhere in the body.
3
Assessment, Contraindications & Safety
Intake, surgical history review, absolute versus relative contraindications, red flags and scope of practice — so you always know when to treat and when to refer out.
4
Core Manual Techniques
Pressure, rhythm and direction of flow, plus the three foundational techniques — stationary circles, pump and scoop — you will use in every protocol that follows.
5
Upper Body Protocols
Full protocols for the neck and terminus, face and head, arms and hands, chest and upper torso. Each one practiced on your own model in the live session, corrected as you go.
6
Lower Body Protocols
Full protocols for the abdomen, lower back and hips, upper legs and inguinal region, lower legs, feet and ankles.
7
Post-Operative Work & Clinical Decisions
Stages of healing, fibrosis awareness and real case studies across liposuction, tummy tuck, BBL and breast procedures — so post-op clients get a practitioner who knows what is normal and what is not.
8
Treatment Planning, Packages & Client Acquisition
Session design from 30 to 120 minutes, planning by client type and SOAP documentation — then the business half: pricing on the value you deliver rather than the clock, where your first clients come from, and the package conversation that turns a first visit into a series booked the same day.
