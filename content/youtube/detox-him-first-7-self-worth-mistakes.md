## Opening hook
"You've tried the teas, the juice cleanses, the 'reset' plans—but you're still bloated, exhausted, and fighting your body. Meanwhile, you're losing sleep over him, eating whatever's fastest between his texts, and running on stress you've stopped noticing. A cleanse can't flush out a pattern. Let's talk about seven self-worth mistakes that can quietly show up in your digestion, your weight, and your energy—and what to choose instead."

## The 7 mistakes

| # | Mistake | Talking point / practical shift |
|---|---------|---------------------------------|
| 1 | Detoxing your body while staying loyal to what drains you. | Ask: "What am I trying to flush out with this cleanse—and is it actually in my body?" Name the relationship, habit, or stress you keep protecting, and set one boundary with it this week. |
| 2 | Living in constant alert mode around his moods. | Notice how waiting for replies, walking on eggshells, or replaying arguments affects your appetite, sleep, and digestion. Build one daily calm-down ritual (slow breathing, a walk after meals, phone away at dinner). |
| 3 | Eating around his schedule instead of your hunger. | Skipped meals, late-night eating, and "whatever's quick" can leave you bloated and drained. Set a basic rhythm: regular meals with protein, fiber, and water—whether or not anyone else is eating. |
| 4 | Using restriction to feel in control when the relationship isn't. | Watch for cleanses, fasting, or cutting food groups as a way to cope with feeling powerless. Choose nourishment over punishment, and get guidance before eliminating foods. |
| 5 | Treating your exhaustion as a character flaw. | Low energy, poor sleep, and cravings are signals to listen to, not proof you lack discipline. Protect sleep and rest as non-negotiables, and track patterns to share with a provider. |
| 6 | Blaming your hormones without getting evaluated. | Self-diagnosing from social media can delay real answers. If you're dealing with ongoing bloating, weight changes, cycle changes, or fatigue, see a qualified health professional for proper testing and care. |
| 7 | Believing you have to earn rest, care, and pleasure. | Ask: "Would I tell a friend she has to be perfect before she deserves care?" Schedule one form of care that isn't a fix—a massage, a bath, a slow meal, time with friends—and receive it without guilt. |
