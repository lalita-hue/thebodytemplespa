## 1. "Is It My Hormones or My Gut?" The Mistake of Guessing From Symptoms

**Promise to the viewer:** You'll stop guessing, learn why these two get confused, and know what to do first.

### Hook (0:00–0:45)
- "Bloated, exhausted, moody, breaking out, holding weight around your middle... You Googled it. Half the internet says hormones, the other half says gut."
- "Here's the truth: guessing from symptoms is why so many women stay stuck for years."
- **[LIVE]** "Comment **H** if you think your issue is hormones, **G** if you think it's your gut. We'll revisit at the end."

### 1. Why the symptoms look the same
- Shared symptom list: fatigue, bloating, cravings, mood swings, acne, poor sleep, weight gain around the belly, irregular or painful cycles.
- Same symptom, different root. Bloating can come from digestion, *or* it can follow your cycle.

### 2. Your gut and hormones talk to each other
- **Estrogen clearance:** your gut helps move used estrogen out. When you're constipated, more of it can get reabsorbed and recirculate.
- **Stress (cortisol):** stress slows digestion *and* throws off your cycle, sleep and cravings.
- **Blood sugar:** spikes and crashes drive cravings, energy dips and mood, and they affect hormone balance.
- **Thyroid:** the gut plays a part in activating thyroid hormone.
- Key line: "It's rarely hormones *or* gut. It's usually a conversation between them."

### 3. The mistake: guessing
- Buying the supplement from the reel. Cutting out whole food groups. Jumping between protocols.
- Why it backfires: you treat the loudest symptom, miss the root, and lose money and time.
- *(your story: a client who guessed wrong, what changed when she stopped guessing)*

### 4. Clues, not a diagnosis
- **Leans gut:** symptoms tied to meals; bloating builds through the day; changes in bowel movements; reactions to certain foods.
- **Leans hormones:** symptoms follow your cycle (worse before your period, mid-cycle shifts); night sweats or hot flashes; sleep disruption at the same time each month.
- **Both:** very common, which is why tracking matters.

### 5. What to do instead
1. **Track for 2–4 weeks:** symptoms, food, cycle day, bowel movements, sleep, stress.
2. **Get real answers:** take your tracker to a provider and ask about proper testing.
3. **Support the foundations that help both:** fiber and hydration, daily bowel movements, balanced blood sugar (protein at every meal), sleep, stress care, movement, lymphatic support.

## 2. Is This Really a Toxic Relationship?

**Promise to the viewer:** You'll know the difference between a hard season and a harmful pattern, and how your body (and his) can make everything feel worse.

### Hook (0:00–0:45)
- "Everything is 'toxic' now. The coworker, the mother-in-law, the husband who forgot the groceries."
- "So how do you know if the relationship is the problem, or if you're running on empty?"
- **[LIVE]** "Comment **D** for 'difficult' or **T** for 'toxic.' What does your gut say about your relationship right now?"

### 1. What "toxic" actually means
- A *pattern* that harms you, not one bad fight.
- Red flags: control, isolation from friends and family, constant criticism or contempt, threats, fear, any physical harm.
- **Safety first:** "If you feel afraid of your partner, this isn't a hormones or gut conversation. Please reach out for help." US: National Domestic Violence Hotline **1-800-799-7233**, or text **START** to **88788**. Put it on screen and in the description.

### 2. Difficult vs. toxic
- **Difficult:** conflict happens, but there's repair, respect and effort on both sides.
- **Toxic:** the same harm repeats, there's no real repair, and you're shrinking to keep the peace.

### 3. When your body is part of the story
- Chronic stress keeps you in fight-or-flight, so neutral things start to feel like threats.
- Poor sleep and blood sugar crashes ("hangry") lower your patience fast.
- Cycle shifts, PMS and perimenopause can change how much you can tolerate.
- **His body counts too:** stress, alcohol, diet and poor sleep affect his mood and reactions. This ties in with *Detox Him First*.
- Key line: "A depleted body can make a good relationship feel toxic, and it can make a toxic one feel normal."

### 4. Self-check questions
- Do I feel safe, physically and emotionally?
- Does this happen even when I'm rested, fed and calm?
- After a fight, is there repair?
- Do I feel like myself around this person?
- When do our fights happen? (cycle day, hunger, exhaustion, after drinking)

### 5. What to do
1. **Regulate first, then decide:** eat, sleep, breathe, and move before the big conversation.
2. **Time hard talks well:** not hungry, not exhausted, not at 11pm.
3. **Get support:** a therapist or coach, and people you trust.
4. **Care for both bodies:** support the nervous system, gut and hormones as a couple.
5. **If it's unsafe:** make a plan with professional help. Don't do it alone.
